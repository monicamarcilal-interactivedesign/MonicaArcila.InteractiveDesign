How I got here

It started with a game. After I presented Hansel & Gretel, my lecturer invited me into his research seedbed: a group of students doing real research with staff. I joined as a developer on an ongoing study of how emotions triggered in VR affect how well people carry out cognitive tasks.

My role

One of three main developers on a shared GitHub project, in Unity. My first task for my teacher and the seedbed was to research haptics and learn how touch relates to emotion. I did not implement the haptics. After that I worked and developed in the project: I built the onboarding tutorial and the emotion questionnaire.

What I did

  1. Haptics research

    My first task. I researched and learnt how vibration relates to emotion, and reviewed the studies in a matrix. This was the groundwork for the team's use of touch in the experience.

  2. Onboarding

    The tutorial that introduces participants to the experience. My first version was a separate tutorial. We later moved it inside the experience, so participants learn while staying immersed.

  3. Emotion check-in

    A short in-app questionnaire with a face scale, from happy to sad. It records how participants feel before they enter the simulation and after they leave, giving the researchers a baseline to compare against.

The research behind the haptics

My notes start from one idea: different vibrations can produce different feelings. I explored the vibro-tactile side of the controllers and headset, and summarised what studies say. One 2025 study I reviewed links each feeling to a pattern of intensity, frequency and duty cycle: happiness is strong, fast and continuous; fear is strong, slow and continuous; relaxation is gentle, fast and intermittent. It also advises calming a negative feeling with a gradual relaxing pattern rather than a happy one.

The studies I worked from: Haptic Vibrations in Emotion Induction and Regulation, 2025 (PDF) ↗ and Haptic feedback in a virtual crowd scenario improves the emotional response, 2023 ↗.

Decisions

  • Decision

    Move the tutorial inside the experience

    A separate tutorial broke immersion. Teaching inside the experience keeps participants in it.

  • Decision

    Measure feelings twice

    Asking before and after gives the researchers a baseline, so they can see what the experience changed.

Status & what's next

The study has been through user testing, and my teammates are gathering the final results. I have no results to show yet.

The seedbed has started a new project on digital avatars in Unreal Engine, where I'm in charge of researching and developing. It has only just begun.
