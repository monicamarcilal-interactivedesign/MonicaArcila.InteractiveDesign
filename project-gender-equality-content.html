Participación, incidencia y diálogo

Social media content design · Instagram carousel · #YoHagoParte · Escuela FEG, Universidad EAFIT

My role
Designer on the School's communications team. I helped put the #YoHagoParte strategy into practice and designed its Instagram content with one teammate
Format
7-slide Instagram carousel with embedded audio quotes

The problem

As part of a month-long institutional campaign (tagged #YoHagoParte) tied to the UN's Agenda 2030 and the 30th anniversary of the Beijing Platform for Action, the brief was to turn a set of recorded interview answers about gender equality — from teaching staff across genders and backgrounds — into something people would actually stop and engage with on Instagram, rather than another static quote graphic.

It was part of a strategy the communications team at EAFIT's School of Finance, Economics and Government (FEG) built together, with the main idea coming from our communications lead. I helped turn it into reality. Each month we published content about women lecturers, administrators, researchers and doctors to make visible the women who work in fields where they are still a minority. We also ran talks and events. At the first event, we handed out handmade wristbands saying #YoHagoParte ("I'm part of it").

Process & solution

The carousel opens with a hook slide — a giant collaged ear and megaphone, torn-paper edges, high-contrast halftone photography — inviting people to "swipe to hear" the conversation, before a context slide grounds the campaign in Agenda 2030 and the Beijing Platform for Action. From there, four slides each pair one interviewee's photo with an embedded audio-waveform player and a pull-quote, moving through different angles on the same theme: the social norms that let gender violence go unreported, the economic cost of excluding half the population from equal work, what active male allyship actually requires beyond agreement, and how digital activism (from the "Ni Una Menos" movement onward) has shifted the conversation toward younger generations. The closing slide frames gender equality explicitly as a collective, ongoing process and invites people to keep following the series.

Visually, every slide uses the same bold system: a torn-paper collage aesthetic, high-contrast black-and-white halftone photography, a light grid background, and a small, consistent neon accent palette (pink, purple, green) — giving four very different interview subjects and topics one unmistakably consistent visual identity across the set.

Result & reflection

The carousel was published on the School's Instagram as part of the #YoHagoParte series. The campaign grew into talks and events, and it used a recurring visual element, origami, that I made for the events. Some of the events went ahead and some didn't. With one teammate I designed the Instagram content, and at one point I led that work.
