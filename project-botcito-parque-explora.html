Botcito for Parque Explora

Mobile app prototype · Design Thinking coursework · Parque Explora, Medellín

My role
Team project. UI/UX design & gamification concept
Client (course project)
Parque Explora, a science park in Medellín, Colombia
Tools
Figma

The problem

Parque Explora is a real science park in Medellín with several practical, everyday visitor needs that usually get handled with plain menus and signage: ordering food at the cafeteria, finding which exhibit rooms suit a visitor's interests, and sorting waste correctly afterwards. The brief was to design a mobile companion experience around Explora's own robot mascot, Botcito, that makes those ordinary tasks feel like part of the park's identity rather than generic app screens bolted onto a science museum.

My role

This was a team project. I designed the UI flows and the gamification concepts across three features: the cafeteria ordering flow, the "what kind of explorer are you?" personality quiz, and the waste-sorting guide.

Process

The prototype covers three connected moments in a visitor's day. First, a QR-triggered cafeteria flow: scanning a code at a table opens a simple menu of the park's food vendors (Mimo's, El Machetico, Dogger, Kon Food and others), each with its own item list and prices, so ordering doesn't require flagging anyone down.

Second, "¡Descubre qué explorador eres!" — a short personality quiz that assigns each visitor one of four robot archetypes, each mapped to a specific room in the park: Neurito (analytical, curious, drawn to questions) for the Mind Room, Mystique (creative, expressive, experimental) for the Stage Room, Tempu (patient, observant, detail-oriented) for the Time Room, and Tamborin (passionate, curious about music) for the Music Room. Framing wayfinding as "which robot are you" turns "where should I go in this park" into something visitors want to answer instead of a room directory they have to read.

Botcito also carries the park's own origin story into a short narrative mini-game: born in his workshop with the mission of generating four different worlds inside this new universe — the city of Medellín — Botcito ventures out and the player guides him through dialogue choices and a side-scrolling level. A two-player button lets a second visitor join and play through levels together, extending the experience beyond a single visitor's phone.

Third, a QR-triggered waste-sorting guide: after finishing a meal, visitors scan a code Botcito displays and get an interactive tray of the waste they're holding (napkins, food scraps, bottles, receipts, cutlery, ice, cardboard...) to sort into the correct one of four categories — organic, recyclable, non-recyclable inorganic, and liquid waste — turning a real after-lunch task into a quick, correctable lesson rather than a wall poster nobody reads.

Solution

One consistent character (Botcito) and one visual system tie together three otherwise unrelated tasks — ordering food, choosing where to go, and sorting rubbish — so the app reads as part of the park's own identity rather than three separate utilities. Each feature uses the same entry point (a QR scan) to keep the interaction low-friction and mobile-first.

Result & reflection

A full, screen-by-screen prototype covering three real visitor needs for a real institution, built around one mascot and one gamification idea (a personality-to-room mapping) that turns wayfinding into something more memorable than a directory.
