What Kind of Animal Would You Be During a Life Crisis?

Branching interactive quiz · DSDN142, Victoria University of Wellington · 2026

My role
Solo — concept, UX writing, interaction design, visual system and AI-assisted build
Tools
Figma Design, Figma Make, Claude, Gemini
Timeframe
13 August – 3 September 2026
  • Context

    A solo project for DSDN142 at Victoria University of Wellington: a decision-driven interactive web experience, built in three weeks.

  • Challenge

    Every choice had to really change the outcome, and the whole thing had to be easy for a first-time visitor to click through.

  • Solution

    A two-path quiz, "What kind of animal would you be during a life crisis?", with five choices per path that tally toward one of three endings.

  • Result

    A fully working experience in Figma Make that meets every point of the brief.

  • Learning

    AI works best as a translation layer for ideas you already have, not as a content generator.

The problem

Looking good wasn't enough. The brief asked for a decision-driven web experience built in Figma and made functional with Figma Make: a landing page, at least five choices to interact with and at least three different endings.

Because it is an interaction design brief, every choice had to genuinely change the tone and the outcome, and a person who had never seen it before had to move through it confidently. The hard part was holding two things together: real branching, and a style simple enough to build with my own skills in the time available.

My role

Solo: concept, writing, interaction design, visual system and build.

  • Wrote the branching narrative for both paths.
  • Designed the interaction model: affordances, feedback and navigation.
  • Built the visual system: colour, type and a small reactive character.
  • Used Claude and Gemini throughout, not to design for me, but to test branching logic, draft and revise choice text, develop the character, and turn what I wanted into the variables and conditional actions Figma Make needs.

The idea

The same emotional mechanism, run twice. At the start you pick which crisis to live through. Both paths have five escalating choices and three endings, but each has its own scenario, tone and animals, so the format itself comments on generational difference.

  • Path 1 · Millennial

    The slow realisation that divorce is an option at 40

    Five choices, from "The Receipt" to "The Last Morning". Endings: Turtle, Wolf and Butterfly.

  • Path 2 · Gen Z

    An entry-level job, a new manager you once ghosted

    Five choices, from an unpaid "opportunity" at onboarding to a competing job offer. Endings: Cat, Possum and Capybara.

Every option quietly tags one of the path's three animals, and the animal with the most tags decides your ending. The choices escalate on a five-level "unhinged" scale, one level per choice.

Process

  1. 13 August · Pure confusion

    I started from a Figma flowchart check with Claude. My first wireframe had "start → decision" nodes with no visible connections and duplicate screens instead of one clean path. I chose to build the Millennial path properly before touching Gen Z, and ideated the starter situations with Claude until the concept felt right.

  2. 28 August · Style and desktop design

    With all five Millennial choices written, I turned to the look. I designed a basic reactive character with Gemini's help to carry a facial expression for each choice, and chose the palette.

  3. 29 August · Structural problems

    A re-check against the rubric showed that choices 1 to 4 weren't affecting the ending, only choice 5 was. I tagged every option to one of three animals and switched to a tally. That meant dropping a fourth possible ending to keep the pattern legible.

  4. 1 September · The second path

    I mirrored the structure into the Gen Z path with a new scenario, grounded in a quick search on what today's twenty-somethings face (including a BBC article) and my own experience of the generation. I asked Claude to check that each animal had a real, current cultural reference behind it, which is how the Capybara of Indifference meme replaced a Raccoon.

  5. 3 September · Feedback

    I finished the Gen Z endings and built the feedback layer in Figma Make: a reactive "echo" line that responds to the exact option you just picked, so the interface always acknowledges your last action even with no visible score.

Decisions and obstacles

  • Decision

    Locked choices

    Once you pick an option there is no going back. It is simpler to build reliably in Figma Make, and it reinforces the weight of each choice, as you can't undo a decision in real life either.

  • Decision

    Visible affordances

    Buttons have a clear rest state (a thin border) and hover state (a solid fill, and the box widens a little), following Norman's idea of visible signifiers. Interactivity reads before the click.

  • Decision

    A character that reacts

    When you pick an option, everything blurs except an avatar and a reaction bubble. The same line follows you onto the next screen as a plain italic reminder, so a choice's emotional consequence stays visible one screen longer.

  • Decision

    A colour palette that feels a little alarming

    A near-black background with saturated neon pink, cyan and lime, closer to a warning label than "clean and modern", so the look escalates the way the choices do.

  • Decision

    Two voices

    A sarcastic, TikTok-style voice for the reaction lines between choices, like a funny, honest friend who judges your life, and a calmer, documentary voice for the final reveal, so the humour and the result don't blur together.

  • Obstacle

    Choices that didn't matter

    Only the last choice affected the ending, until the rubric re-check on 29 August. The tally system fixed it.

  • Obstacle

    Hours spent on work Figma Make could do

    I built the desktop layout by hand and learnt later that Figma Make could have done it in minutes. Next time I would try the tool first.

Result & reflection

  • 2paths, one per generation
  • 5escalating choices per path
  • 3endings per path
  • 3 wks13 August – 3 September

A fully functional two-path experience in Figma Make that meets every point of the brief: a landing page, five choices per path and three endings, earned by the path taken rather than decided by the last click.

The Millennial path tallies 6 Turtle, 5 Wolf and 4 Butterfly. I kept it slightly uneven on purpose, because a perfect split would have felt mechanical. The Gen Z path came out at 5, 5 and 5, because each animal stays consistent to its own logic.

My biggest lesson was to use AI as a translation layer, not a content generator. Claude and Gemini were most useful for turning a structure already in my head into the exact language Figma Make needed, and for testing whether a branching pattern held together before I built it.

How I prompted Figma Make, with an example

For the reactions after each choice, I told Figma Make that every situation has three options, and gave it, for each option, the exact text for the character's bubble and for the line on the next screen, with a reference screenshot attached.
