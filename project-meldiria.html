Meldiria

Narrative universe & 2D videogame · Universos Narrativos + Animación 2D + Lenguajes de Programación, Universidad EAFIT · 2024

Award
Most Creative Project, Inventiva 2024
My role
Team of 6. I took the lead on game development, co-wrote the world bible (Mythos/Ethos/Topos) and kept it consistent
Tools
Unity · AI-assisted scripting and brainstorming
Deliverables
World bible, 2D videogame, 2D animation, artbook, personality quiz

The problem

Meldiria needed to work as more than a setting for one game — it had to hold up as a transmedia universe, feeding a 2D videogame, a full artbook, a "Biblia de Mundo," and a personality quiz, all from the same source of truth. That meant the world needed genuine internal consistency: not just a good story, but its own physics, its own economy, its own legal system and social structure, specific enough that every other deliverable could be built from it without contradicting itself.

My role

At EAFIT you can combine three courses into one project. Instead of making three separate pieces, our team of six built one universe in Universos Narrativos (Narrative Universes) and used it as the base for Animación 2D (2D Animation) and Lenguajes de Programación (Programming Languages). Teammates who love animation led the 2D animation. I focused on two things.

The world. I wrote the Mythos, Ethos and Topos that define how Meldiria's characters behave in their own contexts. I also did the less visible work of a "rule of three", linking every narrative element to others so that even the smallest detail makes sense inside the world. I proposed new, Meldiria-specific names for the three sections, based on musical terms like "clave" (clef), "contrapunto" (counterpoint) and "homorritmia" (homorhythm). I then wrote, checked and laid out the full world bible, the Biblia de Mundo. I used AI to brainstorm and to check my logic for gaps, but every choice had a reason I could explain.

The game. This was my first time as a developer, and I took the lead on the game, developing it alongside one teammate. I wrote the scripts, checked every mechanic against our project outline, asked teammates for the assets, animations and interfaces we needed, and debugged problems with our lecturer, online research and AI. I also made the final build. This is the project that made me want to keep building games.

Process

Mythos established the founding story and its cast: the Meldirianos, a society split into four social roles (architects, farmers, artisans and guardians) each moving through three life stages (child, adult, sage) across a 100–120 year lifespan, and Dizo, a cosmic-horror antagonist — a CMYK-coloured jester who destabilises Meldiria by dragging its people into an incomprehensible fourth mental dimension. The central legend, that an ocarina hides the key to decoding Dizo's CMYK "ecoires" (echo-waves), sends a farmer-explorer through the world's biomes, predators and Dizo's own mental interceptions — an escalating structure written to double as the game's level progression.

Ethos defined the society's values, both explicit and implicit — community before individual, perseverance, loyalty, cyclical renewal — down to specific, game-ready rules: guardians must account honestly for their share of "polvillo mágico" (magic dust) or face defined social, spiritual or legal sanctions, each with its own restorative- justice logic (restore balance, re-educate, keep the punishment proportionate).

Topos grounded all of it in eight distinct physical settings — the Central Plaza and its Tree of Birth, homes near and far from it, the farmers' homes, the Risveliato study centre, the forests, the sound-dead plains full of predators, and the Aurelis mountain range, where magic-generating winds occur every six months — alongside a fully invented physics system: "ecoire" waves (standing in for light) driven by a black sun and a moon's gravity, producing named weather phenomena like windy storms, "inverse rain," velvet lakes and lightning.

On top of that, the team built out legal, political, economic and technological systems — rights, obligations and sanctions; four ruling councils (Sages, Guardians, Artisans, Farmers) plus a community assembly; an education system of guided excursions from age 15 to 25; and a barter-based harvest economy — so that nearly any scene in the eventual game had rules to check itself against.

Solution

A single, structured world bible that the rest of Meldiria's deliverables draw from directly: the 2D videogame's plot and levels, the artbook's visual language, and a personality quiz that assigns players one of Meldiria's own social roles (architect, farmer, artisan or guardian) instead of a generic, disconnected result.

Result & reflection

Meldiria was nominated at Inventiva, an EAFIT showcase for creativity, innovation and technology, and won Most Creative Project 2024. We presented the game and used the personality quiz to draw people in, and we dressed up as Meldirianos, antennae included. We later collected everything the universe produced into a media portfolio, the "Mapa de Plataformas" (Platform Map).

One thing I learnt: some of my working documents are still visible drafts, with notes still to discuss and a glossary that isn't finished. That's how worldbuilding really works. Knowing which parts of a system are still undecided is as much part of the method as the parts that are done.
