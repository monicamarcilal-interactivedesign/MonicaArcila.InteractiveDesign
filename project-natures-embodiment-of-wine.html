Nature's Embodiment of Wine

Themed AI image generator · Generative Graphic Design COMD 342, Victoria University of Wellington · 2026

My role
Solo — concept, prompt engineering & curation
Tools
Gemini (3.6 Extended Thinking)
Date
August 2026
  • Context

    A solo project for Generative Graphic Design (COMD 342) at Victoria University of Wellington, August 2026.

  • Challenge

    Design one prompt "recipe" that produces a whole grid with a shared identity, and avoid "10,000 bowls of oatmeal": outputs that differ in theory but look the same.

  • Solution

    Animals as portraits of wine in a gentle Burlesque style, controlled by constraining each repeated failure one at a time.

  • Result

    A grid where each animal and wine pairing is its own character within one staging system.

  • Learning

    Don't add more instructions. Find the failure that repeats and constrain only that.

The problem

A generator can make thousands of images that all look the same. The brief was to design a themed generative system: one prompt recipe that produces a grid of images with a shared identity. Kate Compton calls the trap the "10,000 Bowls of Oatmeal" problem: thousands of mathematically unique outputs that a viewer still sees as "a lot of oatmeal".

My own process ran straight into it.

My role

Solo: concept, prompts, diagnosis and curation. I developed the concept, wrote and diagnosed every iteration of the prompt, and curated the final grid, all with Gemini.

The idea

Animals as portraits of wine, in a Burlesque style. It sits where three things I like meet: the variety of animal species, wine, and the Burlesque aesthetic, which could give each pairing a strong personality.

I moved the tone away from an early, flirtatious version towards something gentler, closer to imagining a pet in a costume than anything seductive.

Process

  1. More detail made it worse

    About fifteen prompts in, I thought being very specific (exact expressions, framing, accessories) would give more variety. The opposite happened: the images repeated the same background, the same pose and the same attempt to spell the wine's name on the animal.

  2. Constrain the failures, not the outcome

    I isolated each repeated failure (background, pose, literal text) and added a narrow constraint against just that one, leaving the animal's personality and posture open for the model to interpret.

  3. Make "realistic" measurable

    "The animal's natural anatomy must not be changed more than 80%." The animals stay recognisable, with a defined margin of about 20% to show personality through physical detail. A percentage is something I could check the output against.

  4. One test, one line

    Tested on a tortoise embodying a bottle of Port, an earlier version put lettering on the shell. Adding "avoid at all costs including letters or words in the animal" fixed it: same tortoise, same props (a vest, a chair, a gramophone), with personality carried by pose and staging.

The final recipe

"…generate a quite unrealistic image of an [animal] embodying and expressing in their character a bottle of [wine] wine… soft diffused lighting, velvet drapery, warm gold and deep red tones, vintage cabaret staging… the animal's natural anatomy must not be changed more than 80%, it must be recognisable, so no forced facial expressions or human features… avoid at all costs including letters or words… personality is expressed through pose, props and attitude, not through altering the animal's face." Two variables, animal and wine, run through one fixed staging system.

Result & reflection

  • ~15prompt iterations before the fix
  • 2variables: animal and wine
  • 80%cap on changing the animal's anatomy
  • 1line removed the lettering

Each pairing reads as the same kind of animal, in one coherent Burlesque system. Each still carries its own idea: a porcupine reclining like royalty, a cobra in a top hat, a jaguar draped over velvet, a chameleon holding a cocktail glass.

The clearest lesson: fixing a "10,000 Bowls of Oatmeal" problem isn't about more instructions. It's about finding exactly which failure repeats, constraining only that, and checking each fix against a visible before and after.
