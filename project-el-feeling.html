El Feeling

E-commerce prototype, UI kit & brand manual · Reto Medialab 3 (Interfaces), Universidad EAFIT

My role
Team of 5. I did the information architecture and user needs, and designed the Classes section with its animation
Tools
Figma
Client (course project)
El Feeling, a roller-skate teacher's classes, custom gear and merch
  • Context

    A course project for a real client: a Medellín roller-skate teacher whose classes, custom gear and merch lived only on Instagram. Team of five.

  • Challenge

    Organise everything clearly so booking a class or buying is easy, without losing the brand's tone.

  • Solution

    A clickable Figma e-commerce prototype with an information architecture, a brand manual and a UI kit.

  • Result

    We met the course goals, as our professor confirmed, and the brand's look was extended without losing its identity. The client lost interest by the end, and the project ended there.

  • Learning

    Decide how the content is organised before designing how it looks.

The problem

Great content, no home for it. El Feeling is run by a roller-skate teacher in Medellín. Her classes, custom gear and merch lived only on Instagram, which made them hard to browse, book or buy.

Our brief was to design an e-commerce prototype that organises all of it clearly and makes booking a class or buying something easy, while keeping the brand's existing tone.

My role

The structural side of a team of five.

  • Organised and analysed the information architecture.
  • Worked out the user's needs and how the services and products relate.
  • Helped plan the schedule.
  • Defined interactions and card components for the UI kit, as a base for the more visual teammates to refine.
  • Designed the whole Classes section, with its animation and a responsive version.
  • Helped put the final prototype together.

Process

  1. Listen to the client and the community

    We gathered the client's requirements and surveyed Medellín's roller-skate community. One teammate skates in her classes, which helped us reach people.

  2. Know who we design for

    The survey showed us the audience. We turned it into a user archetype, a 21-year-old part-time student who skates three or four times a week and learns tricks from Instagram and TikTok.

  3. Benchmark and structure

    We looked at brands like Cloudworldwide, WhyNot and Clap Burgers, then built the information architecture and a brand manual that extends her existing identity.

  4. Build the UI kit and the pages

    A kit covering the index, layout grid, desktop and mobile type scales, buttons, cards and navigation. Then individual pages, and finally the interactions and animations, on a plan that ran from February to May.

What the community survey told us
  • Mostly women aged 17–26, studying and working, and comfortable paying online.
  • They use their phones most, so the site had to be fully responsive.
  • Instagram and TikTok are their main channels, so they expect visual, quick, direct content.
  • A strong sense of community; skating is emotional release and personal growth for them.

Decisions

  • Decision

    Structure before style

    I defined the architecture and the card and interaction rules first, so the visual teammates could vectorise and refine on top of a clear base.

  • Decision

    Book without leaving the page

    The Classes page uses a pop-up booking flow, so someone can go from discovering a class to booking it in one place, on desktop and on a phone.

  • Decision

    A shop that isn't only a shop

    The community values connection and well-being, so the site shares those values and buying isn't its only goal.

Solution

A clickable e-commerce prototype in Figma built on the information architecture, brand manual and UI kit. It includes a Classes page for desktop and mobile and a pop-up booking flow.

Result & reflection

  • 5people on the team
  • 4months of planned work, February to May
  • 2layouts for Classes: desktop and mobile

We met the project's goals, and our professor confirmed it. The prototype covers the client's needs and expands the brand's look without losing its identity, and the site reflects the skating community's values.

The client lost interest. By the end she wasn't as keen as at the start to use the prototype or to keep working with us on a better website for herself, so the project ended there.

Honestly, this is the part I would change. A website only matters if the person it's for really wants it, and I think we hadn't kept her involved enough along the way. Next time I would show her small pieces earlier and ask what she would actually use, so we build it with her, not just for her.
