3. Does the mark say M A?

Honest answer: partly. Your mark is an abstract symbol with the letters hidden inside it, which works well as long as it always sits next to your name.

What people will see

The M is easy: two arches with a peak between them. The A is the harder letter: its point is the central peak, but its legs run into the loops. So most people will see a lotus, a bird or a knot first, and find the letters when you point them out.

That is not a flaw. It makes the mark a small secret you can tell: two letters, one line, one light.

If you want M A at first glance

  • Keep it and tell the secret (0 h). Show your M and A trace next to the mark in the brand story. My recommendation for now.
  • Add a letter shortcut (about 1 h). A small MA set in Sora for avatars and favicons, with the full mark on larger versions.
  • Redraw it (2 to 3 h, after submission). Rebuild the line with the pen tool so the A's crossbar is explicit, and keep the dot.

The three versions you made

I would use the solid version (top right) for anything under about 64 px (avatar, favicon): it keeps the two arches readable when small. The line version is for large uses, like the lockups above. The two-tone version, with its darker central stroke, makes the A's leg stand out, so it is the best one for explaining the letters.

This is my reading of your three files, not a rule. Test them at 32 px before choosing.

6. Type and voice

Two fonts you already own on the site, used with a rule: Sora does the work, the handwriting says one short thing.

Sora ExtraBold, 800

Say it plainly.

Sora Regular for every paragraph: clear, even, easy to read at length. Spaced capitals for small labels.

Over the Rainbow: one short line, never a paragraph

Voice, from your own outline

  • Analytical: say what the data can and cannot show.
  • Narrative: tell what happened, in order, as a person.
  • Conversational: short sentences; write to one reader.
  • Clear for the average person: explain, don't show off.

What to avoid (your own notes)

  • Neon or thin type on anything long.
  • Space or astronaut imagery.
  • Claims you cannot back up with a project.

9. How it relates to the portfolio

The portfolio is the stage

Dark, lit, full of movement. It shows the work. Neon, glass and orbs belong here.

The brand is the person

Daylight by default, calm, made for reading and for printing. It shows how I think. It shares the logo, Sora, the handwriting, the blue and the spark with the stage, so the two are clearly the same person.

You do not have to choose one skin: Daylight is the brand's home, Night is the same brand on the portfolio's stage. Use Night for screens next to the portfolio and Daylight for anything that is read or printed.
