Nuri's Greenhouse

VR environment design · Virtual Reality Studio, Victoria University of Wellington · 2026

My role
Solo — environment storytelling, spatial narrative design & audio direction
Tools
Unreal Engine (my first project in it) · Meta Quest
Format
Room-scale VR, headset-based

The problem

For this Virtual Reality Studio piece, the challenge I set myself was to make a character the player never sees feel completely present — entirely through what she left behind. Nuri is an unseen celestial botanical weaver who sustains her greenhouse by turning light and human thought into pigment and thread. The environment had to carry her entire story — who she is, what she does, and the emotional weight of her sudden absence — with no dialogue and no visible character at all.

On top of that, the piece needed a real emotional arc: not a series of disconnected beats, but one continuous movement from nostalgia and emptiness through curiosity into awe, tracked precisely to the player's own progress through the space.

My role

I designed the environmental storytelling, the spatial layout, the prop placement and the spatial audio direction, and wrote the verbal onboarding that sets up the player's role before they put the headset on: one of the many thoughts Nuri once helped, returning — without realising it — to the space that shaped it. I built the whole experience in Unreal Engine, from the greybox to the final build. It was my first project in the engine, after about three semesters working in Unity.

Process

The environment is built as three connected zones — beginning, transition and destination — each telling a different stage of Nuri's story through props and ambience rather than text. The beginning uses cold light, moss, an open journal, loose paper and a cold cup of tea with flies around it to establish absence and the suddenness of her departure, without a single explicit clue. The transition introduces jars of pigment and spools of thread, revealing how she worked. The destination holds an incomplete embroidery frame with a broken weave and a visibly missing piece — what was left unfinished when she had to leave.

Spatial audio reinforces the same arc directly: ambient sound is eerie and sparse in the first zone, growing louder as the player follows a golden thread toward the embroidery, culminating in a spatialised moment of connection with the story.

  1. Greybox → final

    The biggest change between greybox and the final build was moving from an open, boundary-less sky concept to a grounded, walled greenhouse. The open-sky version made it nearly impossible to define clear explorable boundaries or give the player a stable ground reference; enclosing the space solved both problems while keeping the same emotional core, and let me reshape the narrative slightly to read more clearly.

Solution

A three-zone VR greenhouse where the story is told entirely through space, props and sound. The player is verbally onboarded before putting on the headset, which defines both their role and their agency: they cannot change the outcome of Nuri's story, but they can decipher — with guidance — what might reverse the greenhouse's decay.

The climax is a physical, embodied act rather than an observed one: the player's own hand connecting the missing potion to the final constellation is what undoes Nuri's abandonment of the greenhouse. Walking between the enclosed greenhouse and a dome open to outer space lets scale communicate something a flat screen could only describe, not make you feel — which is the reason this story needed VR specifically, not just a nice-to-have format for it.

Result & reflection

A complete, walkable VR environment that tells a full character arc without dialogue, narration text, or the character ever appearing — using environmental storytelling, spatial audio and one embodied action as the emotional payoff.
