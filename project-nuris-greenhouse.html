Nuri's Greenhouse

VR environment design · Virtual Reality Studio, Victoria University of Wellington · 2026

My role
Solo — environment storytelling, spatial narrative design & audio direction
Tools
Unreal Engine (my first project in it) · Meta Quest · AI-generated book images · licensed third-party 3D models
Format
Room-scale VR, headset-based
  • Context

    Virtual Reality Studio at Victoria University of Wellington. A solo VR environment.

  • Challenge

    Make a character the player never sees feel present, through what she left behind.

  • Solution

    A walled greenhouse in three zones, told through props, light and spatial audio, ending in one action with the player's own hand.

  • Result

    A walkable environment tested by two teachers and a classmate. They read the character well but got lost at the potions and found the book text hard to read.

  • Learning

    Text in VR needs far fewer words. My first Unreal project taught me to design for boundaries and a stable ground.

The problem

Make someone feel present without ever showing her. Nuri is an unseen celestial botanical weaver who keeps her greenhouse alive by turning light and human thoughts into pigment and thread. The environment had to tell her whole story (who she is, what she does, the weight of her sudden absence) with no dialogue and no visible character.

It also needed one continuous emotional movement, from nostalgia and emptiness, through curiosity, into awe, tied to the player's own progress through the space.

My role

Solo designer and builder. I designed the environmental storytelling, the layout, the prop placement and the spatial audio, and wrote the verbal onboarding that sets the player's role before the headset goes on: one of the many thoughts Nuri once helped, returning without realising it to the space that shaped it.

I built it in Unreal Engine from greybox to final. It was my first project in the engine, after about three semesters in Unity.

How I used AI, and what isn't mine

Images. Gemini generated the images in the book and on the sheets of paper, because I'm not a drawer and fitting 3D models were hard to find for this character. I iterated on the results and gave it reference images to keep them consistent with the space.

Technical help. While learning Unreal I used it as an assistant, for example by sending screenshots of Blueprints and error logs. The headset connection kept dropping and I only had short windows to work.

Assets. The 3D models and sound effects are licensed third-party assets, credited in my asset list.

How it works

  1. Beginning · absence

    Cold light and moss. An open journal, loose paper and a cold cup of tea with flies around it. You infer her habits and how suddenly she left, without a single explicit clue.

  2. Transition · how she worked

    Jars of pigment and spools of thread show her craft.

  3. Destination · what was left

    An incomplete embroidery frame with a broken weave and a visibly missing piece.

  4. Sound that follows you

    Ambient sound is eerie and sparse at first, then grows louder as you follow a golden thread toward the embroidery. The thread lights up and glows where you step, dust drifts in the air and grabbing an object has its own sound.

Decisions and obstacles

  • Decision

    From open sky to a walled greenhouse

    The open-sky greybox made it nearly impossible to define explorable boundaries or give the player stable ground. Enclosing the space solved both, kept the emotional core and let me reshape the story to read more clearly.

  • Decision

    Why this needed VR

    The climax is physical: the player's own hand connects the missing potion to the final constellation. Walking from the greenhouse to a dome open to space lets scale say what a flat screen could only describe.

  • Obstacle

    A new engine and an unstable headset

    Switching from Unity to Unreal while the headset connection kept failing. I kept moving by getting precise answers in the short windows when everything worked.

Solution

A three-zone VR greenhouse told mostly through space, props and sound, with a few illustrated book pages and papers.

The player is onboarded verbally before putting the headset on. They cannot change the outcome of Nuri's story, but they can decipher, with guidance, what might reverse the greenhouse's decay. Their own hand undoes her abandonment of the greenhouse.

Result & reflection

  • 3connected zones
  • 3people played it: two teachers and a classmate
  • 1designer and builder

A complete, walkable VR environment that tells a character arc without dialogue or the character ever appearing.

Players worked out who the character was and the main context, though not the whole story. Some got lost in the potions area, because they had to guess which potion opens the path. They also suggested putting less text on the images.

That matched what surprised me. The images were sharp, but in VR your hands shake and the resolution keeps adjusting to where you look, so the text was hard to read. The dust, the grab sounds, the spatial audio that grows as you get closer and the glowing thread all worked well.

This was a solo project, so I didn't keep a journal or a greybox recording. If I continued it, I would cut the book text down and make the path-opening potion easier to find.
