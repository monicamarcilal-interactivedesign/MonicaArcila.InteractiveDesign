Ciudades Posibles

Speculative urban research & interactive prototype · Research seedbed (Semillero Cosmogénesis), Universidad EAFIT · RedColsi

My role
Team of 10. In phase 3 I co-designed the hexagon pieces, ran interviews, designed a floor guide for visitors, and worked on the palette and poster
Methods
Qualitative surveys, speculative design, participatory prototyping
Site
Distrito SUR, Medellín (El Poblado–Aguacatala corridor)
Context
Research seedbed project alongside my degree, not part of any course

The problem

Medellín's Distrito SUR — the corridor between the El Poblado and Aguacatala metro stations — holds two starkly different versions of the city side by side. El Poblado, with gated parks and surveillance, has 18m² of effective public space per resident; Guayabal, industrial and residential, has only 8m². Colombian law (Decreto 1077 de 2015) requires a minimum of 15m² per inhabitant, but in practice, distribution follows market logic rather than spatial-justice principles — and official 2030 projections show El Poblado's population growing while Guayabal's shrinks from 94,470 (2015) to 62,086. The question the project set out to answer: how do you make public space feel like it belongs to everyone, not just plan it on paper?

My role

A ten-person team project developed within Semillero Cosmogénesis, an EAFIT research seedbed, and presented through RedColsi, under coordinating professor Isaac Montoya López and associate professor Andrea Cuenca. It was work alongside my degree, not part of any course. After Meldiria, our Universos Narrativos lecturer invited my teammates and me into the seedbed to help imagine futures for a district of Medellín.

I worked on phase 3. With a friend, I developed the hexagon pieces for the experience, including their sizes and the possible spaces they represent. I interviewed people and helped with a survey so that what we built responded to real needs. The project changed week to week, so I also joined the design-thinking discussions where the team made decisions. I designed a physical floor guide that showed visitors the steps of the experience. It wasn't used the way we intended, which taught me a lot about testing wayfinding with real people. I also worked on the initial colour palette with a teammate and helped design the final poster.

Process

We ran qualitative surveys with people passing through Las Vegas, one of the corridor's key streets, asking how safe they felt in local public space, what activities they wished it supported, and what would improve their sense of security. Most respondents felt only moderately safe; outdoor sport, cultural events and markets were the most-wanted uses, and better lighting plus more community/police presence were the top asks. From that data we built a user archetype, Laura, a 22-year-old university student from Envigado who wants a walkable, safe, sustainable city and would take part in an interactive experience exploring future-city proposals.

The research and design work was structured around Semillero Cosmogénesis's own worldbuilding framework — Mythos, Ethos and Topos — the same lens the group uses for fictional universes, applied here to a real place instead. Mythos frames El Poblado and Guayabal's contrast as mythological: a struggle between a city planned from capital and one emerging from popular desire. Topos grounds that story in the specific territory — the metro corridor itself, hidden streams like La Volcana, and the urban voids that could become plazas or community gardens. Ethos sets the project's four ethical pillars: participation as belonging (drawing on Hartmut Rosa's theory of resonance — a space is only truly public when it "speaks to us, touches us, transforms us"), felt spatial justice (treating the legal 15m² minimum as a floor, not a ceiling, decided through community co-decision), affective sustainability (recovering streams and sidewalks as emotional refuge, not just green infrastructure), and mobility with sensitivity (human-scale movement over car-centric design).

Methodologically, the project moved through a sequence of interactive stations — Introspección (activating participants' imagination through conversation about the El Poblado/Guayabal contrast), Fundamentación (connecting that imagination to critical urbanist references: tactical urbanism, urban ecology, the "15-minute city"), and Construcción (turning the imagined city into something physical and shared) — grounded throughout in Lubomír Doležel's idea of fictional worlds as "ensembles of non-actualised possible states," used here as a methodology for imagining real urban futures, not just fictional ones.

Solution

The Construcción stage produced a physical, tabletop prototype: a rotating map with physical pieces that let a participant make affective urban decisions for the Distrito SUR, designed as a pilot experience that could circulate through local schools and institutions in comunas 14 and 15 (El Poblado and Guayabal) — turning an abstract policy conversation about square metres of public space into something people could sit down and actually decide on together.

Result & reflection

A research poster and interactive prototype connecting real urban policy (Decreto 1077 de 2015), real survey data from the neighbourhood, and a speculative-fiction methodology borrowed from narrative worldbuilding — evidence that the Mythos/Ethos/ Topos framework the seedbed built for fictional universes holds up just as well applied to a real, contested piece of a city.

Methods

  • Speculative design
