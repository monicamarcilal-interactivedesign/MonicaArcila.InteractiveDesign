Ciudades Posibles

Speculative urban research & interactive prototype · Research seedbed (Semillero Cosmogénesis), Universidad EAFIT · RedColsi

My role
Team of 10. In phase 3 I co-designed the hexagon pieces with a friend, ran interviews with a teammate, designed a floor guide for visitors on my own, made the colour palette with a teammate, and helped teammates with the poster
Methods
Qualitative surveys, speculative design, participatory prototyping
Site
Distrito SUR, Medellín (El Poblado–Aguacatala corridor)
Context
Research seedbed project alongside my degree, not part of any course
  • Context

    A research seedbed project at Universidad EAFIT (Semillero Cosmogénesis), outside any course. A team of ten, presented through RedColsi.

  • Challenge

    Make public space feel like it belongs to everyone in a divided part of Medellín, not just plan it on paper.

  • Solution

    An interactive experience in three stations that ends in a tabletop map with physical pieces where visitors make decisions together.

  • Result

    A research poster and a pilot prototype that visitors tried at a stand, made for schools in two comunas of Medellín. My floor guide wasn't used as we intended.

  • Learning

    Test wayfinding with real people, because a guide that makes sense to you may not work for a visitor.

The problem

  • 18 m²effective public space per person in El Poblado
  • 8 m²per person in Guayabal
  • 15 m²the legal minimum (Decreto 1077 of 2015)
  • 94,470 → 62,086Guayabal's population, 2015 to the 2030 projection

Two versions of one city, side by side. The corridor between the El Poblado and Aguacatala metro stations holds both. El Poblado has gated parks and surveillance. Guayabal is industrial and residential, with little green space.

The law asks for 15 m² of public space per person, but in practice it is shared out by market logic, not by spatial justice. The question: how do you make public space feel like it belongs to everyone, not just plan it on paper?

My role

A team of ten in a research seedbed, working on phase 3. The seedbed is Semillero Cosmogénesis, with coordinating professor Isaac Montoya López and associate professor Andrea Cuenca. It was work alongside my degree, not part of a course. After Meldiria, our Universos Narrativos lecturer invited my teammates and me to help imagine futures for a district of Medellín.

  • Took part from the beginning: I went to about 90% of the meetings, offering help and company wherever the team needed it. My main emphasis was in the second-to-last phase.
  • With a friend, developed the hexagon pieces for the experience, including their sizes and the spaces they represent.
  • With a teammate, interviewed people and helped with a survey, so what we built answered real needs.
  • Joined the weekly design-thinking discussions where the team made decisions, since the project changed week to week.
  • Designed a physical floor guide that showed visitors the steps of the experience.
  • Made the first colour palette with a teammate, and helped teammates with the final poster (I was not its main author).

Listening first

We asked people on Las Vegas, a key street in the corridor. How safe do they feel in public space, what would they like to do there, and what would make them feel safer?

Most felt only moderately safe. Outdoor sport, cultural events and markets were the most wanted uses, and better lighting plus more community and police presence were the top asks. From this we built a user archetype, Laura: a 22-year-old university student from Envigado who wants a walkable, safe, sustainable city.

Mythos, Ethos, Topos

The seedbed's worldbuilding framework, the one used for fictional universes, applied to a real place.

  • Mythos · the story

    A struggle between two ideas of a city

    El Poblado and Guayabal's contrast is mythological: a city planned from capital against one that emerges from popular desire.

  • Topos · the place

    A real territory

    The metro corridor, hidden streams like La Volcana, and urban voids that could become plazas or community gardens.

  • Ethos · the values

    Three ethical pillars

    Participation as belonging, after Hartmut Rosa: a space is only public if it "speaks to us, touches us, transforms us". Felt spatial justice: the legal 15 m² is a floor, not a ceiling, decided together. Affective sustainability and mobility with sensitivity: streams and sidewalks as emotional refuge, and human-scale movement.

The experience

  1. Introspección

    A conversation about the El Poblado and Guayabal contrast activates the participant's imagination.

  2. Fundamentación

    That imagination meets critical references: tactical urbanism, urban ecology, the "15-minute city". Lubomír Doležel's idea of fictional worlds as "ensembles of non-actualised possible states" is used here as a method for imagining real urban futures.

  3. Construcción

    The imagined city becomes something physical and shared: a rotating tabletop map with pieces that let a participant make affective urban decisions for the district.

My floor guide, and what happened to it

I designed a mat for the floor, starting from a first draft. It opened with a question: how much green public space does a person need to feel part of their city? (The WHO recommends 9 m² per person, and Medellín has 4.8 m².) It also showed visitors the steps of the experience.

It wasn't used the way we intended. That taught me a lot about testing wayfinding with real people before the day.

Solution

A pilot that can travel through schools. The tabletop prototype was designed to circulate through local schools and institutions in comunas 14 and 15 (El Poblado and Guayabal). It turns an abstract policy talk about square metres into something people can sit down and decide on together.

Result & reflection

A research poster and an interactive prototype that connect real policy (Decreto 1077 of 2015), real survey data from the neighbourhood, and a speculative-fiction method borrowed from narrative worldbuilding. It shows that the Mythos, Ethos and Topos framework the seedbed built for fictional universes also works on a real, contested piece of a city.

Methods

  • Qualitative surveys
  • Speculative design
  • Participatory prototyping
